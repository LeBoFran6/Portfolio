The premise

Fishing, but not the relaxing kind. You fish in a heavy, quietly horrific atmosphere, and the whole thing runs on an actual PlayStation 1: 33 MHz, 2 MB of system RAM, 1 MB of VRAM.

That is not a stylistic constraint. It is the machine, and it sets every number that follows.

What no toolchain decides

Unity for scene authoring, SplashEdit for export, the PSXSplash runtime on the console itself, Lua for game logic. That part is solved — the toolchain compiles, packs VRAM and builds the disc image.

What no toolchain decides for you is what fits. That is the actual work on this project.

The budgets

Every scene is budgeted to show as much detail as the hardware allows while holding a stable 30 fps on original hardware. The constraints are concrete:

No headroom for a mistake

None of this is complicated once you know the platform and know your budgets. It is surgical, though, because there is no headroom to absorb a mistake.

On 2 MB of RAM, one texture too large is not a slowdown — it is a scene that does not load.

Result

LASTMEAL builds to a disc image and plays on the console it was written for: 2,000 triangles a scene, 256 × 256 atlases, 30 fps, inside 2 MB of system RAM and 1 MB of VRAM.
On hardware

Runs on an original PlayStation 1, not on an emulator. The disc image is the build output, and the console is the test bed.
