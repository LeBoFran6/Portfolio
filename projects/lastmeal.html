Personal project

LASTMEAL

A fishing game for the original PlayStation, running on real 1994 hardware. Built in Unity with PSXSplash, gameplay scripted in Lua.

The problem

Fishing, but not the relaxing kind. You fish in a heavy, quietly horrific atmosphere, and the whole thing runs on an actual PlayStation 1: 33 MHz, 2 MB of system RAM, 1 MB of VRAM.

The pipeline is Unity for scene authoring, SplashEdit for export, the PSXSplash runtime on the console itself, Lua for game logic. That part is solved — the toolchain compiles, packs VRAM and builds the disc image.

What no toolchain decides for you is what fits. That is the actual work on this project.

What I did

1
Budgeted every scene to the hardwareShowing as much detail as the platform allows while holding a stable 40 fps on original hardware.
2
Treated constraints as design, not compromiseChose what each scene could afford up front rather than optimising after the fact.
3
Built the whole thing as a playable artefactExported to a genuine PS1-compatible .ISO and runs on the console it was written for.
