The problem

A client wanted several live camera angles on screen at once. The first implementation couldn't hold framerate.

The obvious approach is also the expensive one: every extra active camera is another full render pass — culling, shadows, post-process, the lot.

The approach

So I rebuilt the system around Scene Capture 2D components writing to render targets:

What each decision bought

1
Isolated captureEach secondary view captures to its own render target, then composites in UI
2
Decoupled cadenceCapture rate is independent of game framerate — a rear view doesn't need 60 fps
3
Trimmed show flagsNo shadows, no post-process, no fog on secondary views
4
Resolution matched to outputCapture resolution scaled to actual on-screen size, not full res

Result

~13 ms
Frame time with every view live
16.6 ms
60 fps budget — landed inside it, with headroom
12 ms
Of that 13, spent on the render thread
Still render-thread bound though: draw sat at 12 ms of that 13. If I picked this up again, that's exactly where I'd go — instanced static meshes and merged materials on the secondary captures to cut draw call submission.

The thing I keep relearning as a Technical Artist: most performance problems aren't "make the renderer faster." They're "stop rendering what nobody is looking at."

Prototype visuals, no art direction on this one — the only thing worth looking at is the frame time.
